# Introduction

In the fluorescent-lit corridors of a prestigious university’s neuroscience complex, where the coffee is cold, the grants are colder, and the p-value is a god that must always land just below 0.05, a cast of weary graduate students, bewildered post-docs, and over-caffeinated research assistants struggle to survive a system whose logic is perfectly circular. The brilliant coder who can build a deep neural network overnight but cannot say what a neuron is shares a lab with the seasoned electrophysiologist who still fears the “Save As” button. Above them all presides a Principal Investigator who has never mastered a technique, an equation, or a hypothesis, but has mastered the far more valuable arts of networking, grant-speak, and being on the right committee at the right time. As the lab chases publications, funding, and significance in a Catch-22 where one must already have the results to get the money to do the experiments that produce the results, the story spills out of the university into industry, where the findings of academia are turned into something useless or something sinister. Told in a nerdy, invented dialect of techno-babble somewhere between a turbo encabulator and a Clockwork Orange, it is a dark and absurd comedy about how science, in its quest for truth, quietly lost its way.

# Main themes

1. **The absurdity of institutions** — academia, medicine, corporations — when confronted with genuinely transformative discovery. There is a fundamental conflict of interest between the science (truth) aims, and fiduciary obligation of these entities. This confilic is often buried under the bureaucratic absurdity.
2. **Bureaucratic absurdity:** everyone is trapped. If the problem cannot be solved, the powerful men, solve it on paper.
3. **The gap between knowledge and understanding** — you can know everything about the brain and understand nothing about the mind. Also dual nature of having scientist with no computation skills and quants with no science.
4. **The Publish-or-Perish Paradox:** The central satire. The pressure isn't to be *right*, but to be *published*. This drives characters to "p-juice" their data, chase statistically significant but scientifically meaningless results, and frame their work around buzzwords rather than hypotheses.
5. **The comedy of expertise** — everyone is an expert in their narrow field and an idiot in everything else, and the system rewards this.
6. **The tragedy of metrics** — measuring the wrong things perfectly is worse than measuring the right things imperfectly
7. **The humanity hidden in jargon** — beneath all the Neurobabel, the characters are people trying to connect, understand, and survive
8. **Immigration and belonging** — Axel and Azie are caught by a legal and bureaucratic, but it's also existential: where do you belong when you're between countries, between student and professional, between knowing too much and being credentialed too little? They are disowned by their mother society and were not fully accepted by the new one. They are orphans of the global village.
9. **The relationship between language and thought** — Neurobabel isn't just slang; it shapes how the characters think, what they can and can't express, what they can and can't discover.
10. **The underlying grief**: The science is funny. The system is funny. But the people trapped in the system are suffering. Axel's catch is comic in structure and tragic in reality. A brilliant person, unable to contribute, slowly being ground down by a system designed to protect itself, not to discover truth.
11. **The Old vs. The New:** The conflict between brilliant, old-school scientists who can't operate a command line and new-school coders who can build a neural network but don't know what a neuron is. This generational and philosophical gap is a source of both conflict and humor.
12. **The Nature of Intelligence:** With a focus on AI, computational neuroscience, and linguistics, the story constantly asks: What is consciousness? Can it be coded? Is the brain just "wetware"? This provides the intellectual and philosophical depth.

# The Language: *Neurobabel*

Like Burgess's *Nadsat* in *A Clockwork Orange*, the characters speak in a pidgin of neuroscience jargon, coding slang, statistics worship, and academic bureaucracy — used so casually it replaces ordinary language. Call it **Neurobabel** — half Tower-of-Babel, half techno-babble.

### Sample terms:

**Publoaf:** A publication made entirely from recycling fragments of previous publications.

**Bayesplosion:** When Bayesian modeling produces so many probability distributions that no one remembers the original question.

**p-hackify and p-torture:** To “improve” statistical results by cherry-picking or re-running until significance appears.

**Null hypothesis:** a boring person

**Connectome:** a particularly messy thing, like plate of spaghetti.

**Using technical Adjectives for daily life:** *under-powered (describing a penis)*, *over-fitted ()*, *non-significant (describing attractiveness of a girl)*, *computationally expensive* (describing a date).

**Exclamations:** "By Godel's ghost!", "For the love of Turing!", "What in the name of the central limit theorem is that?"

**Fortissimo: Loudly asserting something false to drown out dissent.**

**Desk-rejected:** Shot down instantly by a gatekeeper (journal, funder, dean) without real review.

**Salami-slicing:** Breaking one mediocre study into many tiny publishable fragments.

**CV padding:** Any activity done purely to look productive on paper—committees, scripted “outreach,” meaningless co-authorships.  
 **Bonferroni exorcism:** Aggressively correcting for multiple comparisons to banish any hint of significance.  
**Effect-size cosplay:** Pretending minuscule, unreliable effects are world-changing.  
**Statistical martyrdom** *or Stat jihadi:* Refusing to publish interesting results because the study isn’t rigorous enough.  
**Cortical karaoke:** Streaming words, music, or suggestions directly into someone’s cortex; also any excessively persuasive neuro-presentation.

**Consciousness-as-a-service (CaaS):** A business model: renting slices of attention, awareness, or decision-making to clients.

**Lab mules:** Grad students or residents who do all the labor with none of the credit.  
**Overhead vampire:** University administration absorbing grant money as “indirect costs.”  
**impact factor junkie:** Researcher addicted to publishing only in high-impact journals, regardless of substance.  
**burnout chic**: The culture of wearing exhaustion, cynicism, and overwork as a badge of honor.  
**The Reproducibles:** Cult-like group who remained partially synchronized, devoted to extreme rigor and replication.  
**Add a control group:** Snarky way to say “get perspective” or “you’re biased.

**IRB’d:** Choked to death by beaurocracy.  

**Quantum Neurobollocks: consciousness theories based on quantum physics, specifically, the misunderstood observer effect with consciousness observer effect.**
